Smart Assistant: From Answering to Operating

SmartLead PM assignment · Pratik Prakash

1Summary

What if outbound teams could stop telling Smart Assistant what to do next, and simply tell it what they want to achieve?

Give SA an objective. Let it build the plan, watch it run and fix what breaks. You approve. This brief sets out how SA gets there in 6 months.

2Goal, scope and problem

Goal

Retention, driven by engagement. SmartLead already has ~30K revenue teams, so keeping and growing them matters more than acquiring new ones. Users who get more done through SA stay longer, expansion (more client workspaces, mailboxes and credits) follows that trust, and every plan they approve, edit or undo teaches SA what works, building a data moat over time.

Scope

Smart Assistant over the next 6 months, including what SA owns with SmartAgents (the workflow builder).

The product problem in a nutshell

  • SA approves steps, not plans, so every task turns into back-and-forth
  • SA takes commands, not goals, so users break their work into steps for it
  • SA doesn't guide towards getting the job done, so users have to figure out next steps themselves
  • SA waits to be asked, so breakages are found late and end in a support ticket
  • SA doesn't use the SmartAgents engine, so most of its power stays hidden

3Product strategy

The vision: from "Ask SA" to "Give SA an objective"

The user states an objective ("30 meetings for Client X this month"). SA builds the outbound plan for one approval, watches it run, catches problems early and fixes them, so you hear about problems from SA, not from your client.

Five bets

  1. SA orchestrates the agents SmartLead already has. Prospecting, reply, alerts and automations already exist as SmartAgents; the one gap is an email campaign agent. SA becomes the single place that runs them all.
  2. Do, don't chat. Every answer ends with a usable result, such as a draft campaign or a fix, plus a one-click next step.
  3. Watch by default. Useful checks are on from day one, so problems reach the user before the client.
  4. Trusted, not automatic. SA sends or spends only with approval, and what runs is exactly what was approved. For a company known for deliverability, trust is the better bet.
  5. Answer like a cold-email expert. SA uses what SmartLead sees across thousands of accounts: "Your reply rate is below similar campaigns. The usual cause is your list. Want me to fix it?"

What SA deliberately is not

  • An autonomous AI SDR: the category churns, and autonomous sending puts customers' domains at risk. Sending stays gated, even in Phase 3.
  • A copywriting tool: AI copy is a commodity, and not why users leave.
  • A data or enrichment layer: that's Clay's territory, and Clay is a partner.
  • A new agent engine: we extend SmartAgents with one email campaign agent.
  • Primarily a support deflector: it will reduce tickets, but optimizing for deflection keeps SA reactive.

5Solution roadmap

Phase 1(months 0–2): one front door, one approval

Connect SA to the existing SmartAgents and add the one missing agent. This removes the cost of using SA, and every later phase depends on it: if approval stays per tool call, more capability means more interruptions.

ItemWhat it isEffort*
1a. SA orchestrates SmartAgentsSA runs the existing prospecting, reply, alert and automation agents from one chat. "Keep an eye on X" creates a SmartAgent in plain languageM
1b. Email campaign agentThe one missing agent: drafts the sequence, picks healthy mailboxes and sets the schedule. Built on SmartAgents' plan → test → deploy patternM
1c. Plan-level approval, not tool-levelThe whole plan (leads, sequence, mailboxes, schedule) as one editable card, approved onceM(reuse)
1d. Draft first, ask laterFills defaults from the account and asks questions once, together. Every answer ends with a ready output and a one-click next stepM
1e. Approval gatesDrafts and edits run automatically. Approval only to send (reputation) and spend (credits)S
1f. Three default alertsMailbox disconnected, bounce spike, stuck queue. In-app and email, Slack optional. Detection plus a "Fix with SA" linkS(engine exists)

Fixes need reliable, approved actions, which Phase 1 builds.

Why last: a goal-driven playbook that launches badly or misses breakage loses trust at the largest scale.

The wedge: the first moment a user feels SA changed their work. Today, launching one campaign with SA took me 6 messages. In the wedge, an agency owner types an objective ("Book a webinar with 100 HR leads at mid-size SaaS companies") and gets back one complete, editable plan: the lead list, the sequence, the mailboxes and the schedule. They approve once, and it launches. Target: this happens in the first session.

Reason to come back: SA tells you a mailbox broke before your client notices, with a fix ready for your approval.

6Metrics

★

North star: Active SA work

Campaigns, workflows and fixes that SA built, a user approved, and are still running this week, per account (each agency client counts as one).

Why this number: it counts work in use, not chat (message count rises when SA gets worse), and anything undone or paused drops out.

Guardrails

  • Breadth: share of active accounts with at least one SA item running
  • Effort: messages to finish a task, from about 6 today to 2 or fewer
  • Trust: zero plans that run differently from what was approved
  • Safety: SA campaigns get no more bounces or spam complaints than manual ones; no credits spent without approval

7Business

  • It protects agency revenue. When a campaign breaks quietly and an agency loses a client, SmartLead loses that client's workspace too. At about $29 per white-label client, a 20-client agency pays roughly $580 a month in client fees alone.
  • Free assistant, paid results. SA stays free; actions that already cost credits stay priced, and SA shows the cost before you approve. Agency features (the cross-client brief, auto-fixes, white-label reports) drive plan upgrades. Instantly charges credits for its assistant, so "free assistant, pay for what it does" is a simple sales message.
  • It's cheap to build and run. It extends SmartAgents rather than building a new engine. Checks are simple rules, and the AI only runs when something needs attention.
  • It builds a moat. Every approval, edit and undo, plus deliverability data across the whole customer base, teaches SA what works. Competitors can't copy that with better prompts. It must be anonymized and pooled across accounts, so one client's data never shapes another's campaigns.
